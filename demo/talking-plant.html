How it works

Finding the plant: (1) the photo is shrunk to 1024 px; (2) green pixels are found by colour (hue, saturation, brightness); (3) the largest green shapes are kept as the plant; (4) brown, yellow or very dark pixels are marked as damage. If the background is brown soil (which looks like a lesion), only patches fully inside the green count; on a plain background, brown patches next to the plant count too, and dark pixels count only when the plant surrounds them, so shadows are ignored; (5) the biggest healthy circle is found with a distance map and the face is placed there, away from lesions; (6) the three biggest damaged patches become the red hotspots; (7) each sentence the plant says makes it look at one hotspot. The photo is frozen, so nothing needs tracking and the face cannot slide off the plant.
Voice: audio goes through a Web Audio AnalyserNode; its loudness (about 60 times a second) opens the mouth. The browser's built-in voice cannot be analysed, so for that voice the mouth follows word-boundary events. The health level also sets the voice pace and pitch (the same table planned for Bulbul).
Buttons: Listen uses your microphone (wide eyes and a ring that grows with your voice). Thinking shows the wait while the AI works. Recovered! shows a happy face with confetti.
Limits: damage % is a colour estimate. Large dead patches at the leaf edge, white mildew, brownish healthy edges, and very dark or very bright photos can be missed or mis-counted. Always confirm with an expert.
In the real app: the audio comes from Sarvam Bulbul, the health level from the diagnosis, and Haiku's hotspots can be combined with these colour hotspots. Nothing else changes.
