Talking Plant: Reality Check + AR Lab

We tested Claude Haiku 4.5 on real labelled leaves before building anything. Here is what we found, and how to turn it into a better project.

Measured today

Is the plant a good doctor? Not on its own.

20 labelled PlantVillage tomato leaves (4 each of early blight, late blight, leaf mold, septoria, healthy), Haiku 4.5 via Bedrock, zero-shot.

1 · Reverse Brainstorming

How would we make the plant fail? Then flip each answer.

Confidently wrong
Confidence Guard calibrated on a test set; "I'm not sure" is allowed
One blurry photo
Three-Shot Check-up: whole plant, close-up, underside; voice-coached
Alarms on healthy leaves
Healthy-first rule, two-of-three agreement before raising an alarm
AI invents the pesticide dose
AI names the product; the dose comes from a verified lookup table
Any disease in the world
Ask the crop first; allow only the 5 to 10 diseases that occur locally
Hide the weakness at the fair
Put the numbers on the poster: 25% → X%. The improvement IS the science.
2 · SCAMPER

Rebuild the doctor

Substitute
One-shot guess → a spoken diagnostic conversation: the plant asks 3 to 5 questions and narrows it down. This is where voice truly helps.
Combine
Our own trained classifier finds the disease, Haiku explains it, Bulbul speaks it. Specialist finds, language model explains, voice delivers.
Put to other use
Look-alike Gallery: show the 3 most similar known cases and let the farmer tap "mine looks like this one".
Reverse
The plant interviews the farmer. The farmer is the doctor; the plant is the patient giving symptoms.
Eliminate
Typing, login, menus, and the photo background (segment the leaf first).
Modify
Collect 30 to 50 real field photos per disease in your district and report lab versus field accuracy.

4 · What If Scenarios

Turn weaknesses into the show

  • Stump the Plant: judges try to fool it; a leaderboard of tricks. Every fooled case becomes test data.
  • Two plants talk: the healthy neighbour warns the sick one, which shows disease spread as a short dialogue.
  • Expert review by voice note: uncertain cases go to a local expert and the verified answer returns in the plant's voice.
  • Offline classifier: a small model runs on the phone, the cloud only does the talking.
  • Live accuracy meter on the poster, updated by thumbs up or down from everyone who tries it.
AR lab

AR that actually works on a phone browser

Ranked by reliability. The most dependable AR is the kind that does not depend on the language model.

Tier 1 · any phone SAFE

  • Disease Vision lens: real-time colour segmentation tints lesion pixels red on the live camera, with a live severity %. No AI call, offline, free.
  • Scan Quality HUD: blur and brightness meter, corner brackets, auto-capture when sharp.
  • Ghost alignment: last week's photo as a see-through guide, so before/after photos match and the recovery numbers are valid.
  • Talking avatar: mouth opens with audio volume, droops or perks with severity.
  • Freeze-frame tags: tap a pinned spot and the plant speaks about it. Frozen image means no drifting overlay.

Tier 2 · booth wow CHECK PHONES

  • Poster QR plant: scan the QR and the plant pops out of the poster and greets the judge in their language.
  • 3D Doctor Plant on the table: model-viewer AR (Android Scene Viewer, iPhone Quick Look). Needs 3D model files. Test on the judges' likely phones first.
  • Future Vision: animate the lesion growing over 7 days, labelled as a simulation.
  • Microscope Zoom: animated illustration of spores entering leaf cells, for school judges.

Tier 3 · stretch RISKY

  • Outbreak compass: arrow to the nearest reported outbreak using GPS and compass.
  • Gesture control: open palm to scan, thumbs up to confirm (MediaPipe).
  • Live hotspot tracking on moving video: overlays drift; avoid for the fair.
  • Soil Jar AR: layer lines on a sedimentation jar photo.
